Common questions

SWP calculator FAQ

Plain language answers to the assumptions behind your projection.

What is an SWP?

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan lets you redeem a set amount from an investment at regular intervals. The remaining amount stays invested and may continue to earn returns.

What is the difference between the two calculation modes?

“Find monthly income” estimates a withdrawal for your chosen time period. “Check corpus life” estimates how long the corpus could support a monthly amount you enter.

How does inflation adjustment work?

When enabled, the calculator increases the monthly withdrawal once each year by your chosen inflation rate. That can help preserve purchasing power, but it also draws down the corpus faster.

Does the result include taxes and fund expenses?

No. The projection uses a constant pre tax return and does not deduct taxes, exit loads, expense ratios, or advisory fees. Use a lower expected return if you want a more conservative approximation.

Why can actual results differ?

Markets do not produce the same return every month. Volatility, especially poor returns early in the withdrawal period, can shorten corpus life even when the long term average return looks similar.

What is a safe withdrawal rate?

There is no universal safe rate. It depends on duration, asset mix, inflation, taxes, flexibility, and sequence of returns risk. Treat this result as a scenario to test, not a guarantee.

Important: this is an illustrative estimate, not financial advice.

The calculator assumes a constant return and annual inflation adjustment. Actual investment performance and corpus longevity can differ because of volatility, taxes, fees, changing withdrawals, and sequence of returns risk. Consider consulting a qualified adviser before making an investment or retirement income decision.
